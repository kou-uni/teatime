# teatime v3 — it now measures what the team gave up

*Follows v2 (10/20): "talk, leave the room, come back and judge a seven-dish course."*

## What changed since v2, and why

In v2, the human leaves and the work moves on without them. That half held up. The other half didn't: **what should come back?**

v2 brought back a course of outputs to judge. Week 6 showed me what was wrong with that.

- Of the kinds of uncertainty, uncertainty about *values* is the only one you can't settle by acting, by observing results, or by finding causes. You can only settle it through deliberation. Machines are already fast at the other three.
- Taste does not show up in ratings. It shows up only when someone says what they gave up.

So teatime no longer summarizes or ranks the discussion. It extracts **abandonments**: which value the room gave up, and what it gave it up for. It also flags when nobody said *why*. That rate is the number I now track: a high rate means the room cannot yet name its own standard.

## What comes back now

You get five things:

- a pitch
- a product spec
- a project plan
- feedback for the participants
- **a working prototype**

The rule from v2 still holds: **documents never fill gaps.** Anything the discussion didn't decide is marked "not provided". The prototype has to fill gaps to exist at all, so it lists every assumption it made behind one button.

## Shipped this week (tested end to end on a phone)

- Tap to record, then go have tea. Progress shows step by step, so you can tell "working" from "stuck".
- **"Never mind" button.** It discards the recording and everything made from it, either while recording or mid-processing. It takes two taps, so a 30-minute discussion can't be lost by accident.
- An archive of past discussions, showing abandonments per session.

## A side experiment: which AI does which step

- Steps that need judgment go to Claude: extracting abandonments, writing the documents, deciding assumptions.
- Speech-to-text and the prototype's JavaScript run on a local model.
- I first gave the prototype's screen to the local model. It ignored the decision: the team had agreed to postpone service ideas, and it showed three. I moved the screen back to Claude. **Turning decisions into a screen turned out to be judgment, not mechanical work.**
- About 80% of my AI cost was re-reading context, not output. Starting fresh sessions saved more than handing work to cheaper models.

## Honest status

Tested only on short recordings (under a minute). **Not yet used in a real team discussion.** The test I care about is to read ten sessions of abandonments and say in one line what value that room keeps giving up. That can't start until real sessions exist.

**Would your team lend me one 20–30 minute discussion?** It could be service design or how your team works. Recordings stay on my machine, and you get all five outputs.
